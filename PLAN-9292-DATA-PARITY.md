# The plan: the data and the journeys, closer to 9292

`PLAN-9292-PARITY.md` is about the journey **page**: the twenty things 9292 draws that OVVO did not.
This file is about the half underneath it — what OVVO **knows**, and what it can **work out**. A
screen can be perfect and still be measuring the wrong thing; this is the plan for the thing being
measured.

It was written on **18 November 2026**, by reading the code rather than the documents, because the
documents in this repository disagree with each other about how far the real data got. Every claim
below names the file it came from, so it can be checked instead of believed.

---

## What the steerer has accepted, in his words

On 18 November 2026 he wrote that he **acknowledges and accepts** that these four are not coming:

- **e-tickets**
- **OV-chipkaart balance**
- **subscription advice**
- **operator crowding data**

All four need something no free source offers: a commercial agreement with the carriers, a
traveller's own payment account, or a count of passengers that only the operator holds. None of the
fourteen steps below depends on them, and none of them pretends to stand in for one. Where a screen
would have shown one of these, it keeps saying nothing rather than something invented.

**What that leaves in scope** is everything else 9292 does with data: every stop, every board,
every journey, leaving *or* arriving, by address as well as by station, with real delays, real
platform changes, real cancellations, real service alerts, the line drawn where the line actually
runs, and a price if a free published price exists.

---

## The two rules every step obeys

1. **No card, ever.** Every source below is free and needs no account. A free plan with limits is
   fine; a trial that ends is not.
2. **No invention.** A missing delay is said to be missing. A missing fare is left empty. A search
   with no answer says there is no answer. `src/lib/dataMode.ts` is the switch that enforces the
   second rule, and it stays off in a production build.

---

## Where OVVO actually stands today

Measured on 18 November 2026, from the code, not from a plan written in a previous round.

**Real, working, verified.**

- **The national timetable is in.** 41 operators, **53,612 stops**, 608,596 trips, 11.3 million
  stop times, valid 14 November – 12 December 2026, downloaded and checked by
  `scripts/gtfs/fetch.mjs`.
- **The graph is built from it**, one service day at a time, in two-hour slices, with walking links
  between stops within 400 m (`scripts/gtfs/build-graph.mjs`). A Sunday build holds 1,133,578
  connections and 353,968 walking links, 25 MiB in 17 files.
- **The search is real and runs in the browser** (`src/planner/graph.ts`, `planRealJourneys`), over
  the official timetable, and it agrees with the slower reference search on most journeys.
- **Boards are real for 60 stops**, generated by `scripts/gtfs/build-app-data.mjs` into
  `src/data/generated/boards.ts` (1.6 MB, built 15 November 2026), each row carrying the feed's own
  trip id.
- **Live delays are real** for the trips a board or a journey is showing, read on the server because
  the feed sends no CORS headers (`shared/live-feed.mjs`, `worker/live.js`, `/api/live`).
- **The streets are real**: OpenStreetMap tiles and OpenStreetMap's own foot router, from the browser,
  no key (`src/lib/streetMap.ts`), with a straight line and an admission when the router does not
  answer.

**Not real, and the fourteen steps below are mostly about these.**

- **The picker searches 61 places.** `src/components/journey/StopPicker.tsx` and
  `src/lib/stopSearch.ts` both search `STOPS` — the app's own hand-picked list — while 53,612 stops
  sit in the graph beside them. **This is the biggest gap in the product**, and it is not a data
  question at all: the data is already downloaded.
- **The search leaves at a time; it cannot arrive by one.** `planRealJourneys(day, origin,
  destination, startMinutes)` takes one clock reading and one direction.
- **The preferences are not applied.** Modes, walking distance, maximum changes, step-free and
  bicycle are settings, and the search reads none of them. `PLAN-FREE-ROUTING.md` is explicit that a
  preference must not be shown as applied when it is ignored.
- **The alerts are invented.** `src/data/disruptions.ts` opens with `DEMONSTRATION DATA` and every
  alert in it is a simulated example. The real alert feed (823 messages on 14 November) is not read.
- **The line is not drawn where it runs.** The route drawn between two stops is a path through the
  stops, not the shape of the railway or the road. `shapes.txt` — 238 MB of real geometry in the
  archive — is read by nothing.
- **Fares and crowding are empty**, deliberately: `toJourney` writes `fare: { eur: 0, label: '' }`
  and `realtime: false` rather than guess.
- **Long journeys cost more than the free budget allows.** 12–16 ms against a 10 ms limit, and one
  journey (Utrecht Centraal → Groningen) is **30 minutes late** against the reference search. The
  cause was narrowed and is not yet found; `PLAN-FREE-ROUTING.md` records the search for it honestly.
- **The timetable is built by hand, a week at a time**, in whichever sandbox the agent happened to be
  in. On 12 December 2026 the feed's dates run out and the planner has nothing to answer with.

**One sentence for the gap.** OVVO can plan a real journey between 61 places it chose, leaving at a
time you pick, with real live delays once you are on it; 9292 plans between any two addresses in the
country, either leaving or arriving, with real alerts, a fare, the line drawn where it runs, and a
plan again when your train is cancelled.

## How the sizes are given

The UI plan measured its sizes in minutes, twice, and those numbers are real. **They do not transfer
to this work**, where a step is a pipeline, a data source and a check rather than a component and a
style. So this file uses sittings instead, and says so rather than borrowing a precision it has not
earned:

- **S** — an afternoon or less.
- **M** — a day or two.
- **L** — most of a week.
- **XL** — a week or more, or a week whose end nobody can see yet.

Every size here is an estimate. Any that turns out wrong gets corrected in this file in the same turn
it is discovered, the way `PLAN-FREE-ROUTING.md` corrects its own.

## The fourteen steps

### 1. Every place in the country, in the picker

**9292:** type any stop, station, village, street or landmark in the Netherlands and it is found.
**OVVO today:** 61 places. A traveller who lives in Franeker cannot plan from their own station.

**Build.** A place index generated from the same graph the search already reads: every stop, every
station and every grouped place (the search already groups platforms into places — `placesOf` in
`src/planner/graph.ts`), with its name, its town and its position. One file, built by the timetable
pipeline so it can never drift from the timetable it describes. The picker keeps `rankStops` as its
one ranking function, so the full list and the short list can never disagree about what a query
means.

**Where.** A new builder step beside `scripts/gtfs/build-app-data.mjs`; a loader in
`src/lib/stopSearch.ts`; the picker and the combobox read the index when it is available and fall
back to `STOPS` when it is not.

**Data.** Already on disk. Nothing new is downloaded.
**Size.** L. The build is small; the work is that every screen that assumes a stop is one of 61 has
to be walked and made safe when it is not — the stop page, the board, the favourites, the journey
card's links.
**Done when.** Typing the name of a stop that is not in the app's 61 finds it, plans a journey from
it, and the journey's legs open without a blank screen. Checked with a deliberately obscure stop, not
with Utrecht.

**The risk, named.** Fifty-three thousand places is a few megabytes of JSON on a phone. Measure the
file first; if it is too heavy, shard it by first letter and fetch the shard the traveller is typing
into. Static files are free and unmetered, so sharding costs nothing but a request.

### 2. A board for every stop, not sixty

**9292:** every stop in the country has a departure board.
**OVVO today:** the 60 stops in `src/data/stops.ts`.

**Build.** Boards generated per stop, per day, into static files the app fetches when a stop is
opened — instead of one 1.6 MB TypeScript module holding all of them. Sixteen hundred kilobytes for
sixty stops is 26 KB a stop; fifty-three thousand of those in one file is not a file.

**Where.** `scripts/gtfs/build-app-data.mjs` becomes a sharded builder; the board hook reads a shard.
**Data.** The timetable already on disk.
**Size.** L.
**Done when.** A stop outside the 60 opens with real departures and real trip ids, the live delays
join to them, and the deployed site still fits inside the host's file limits.

**The constraint that shapes it.** Static assets are capped at **20,000 files**, so 53,612 files of
one stop each is not allowed. Shard by the first letters of the stop id — a few hundred files of a
few hundred stops — and fetch the shard on demand.

### 3. Leaving at a time, and arriving by one

**9292:** both. It is how anyone plans an appointment.
**OVVO today:** leaving only (`planRealJourneys` takes `startMinutes`).

**Build.** The same search run backwards over the same graph, which is the standard way and needs no
new data: start from the destination at the arrival time and walk the connections in reverse. The
results screen offers *Leave at* / *Arrive by* above the time, and both are remembered per search.
**Where.** `src/planner/graph.ts`, the search form, the results screen.
**Data.** None.
**Size.** M — the scan is the easy half; proving it agrees with the forward search on ten known
journeys is the half that matters.
**Done when.** "Arrive by 09:00 in Amsterdam" returns journeys that all arrive at or before 09:00,
and the same journey found forwards and backwards has the same legs.

### 4. Preferences that are actually applied, or said not to be

**9292:** modes, walking pace, fewer changes, step-free, bicycle — and it honours them.
**OVVO today:** the controls exist and the search reads none of them.

**Build.** Apply what the graph can support without new data: which modes are allowed, the maximum
number of changes, walking speed and the longest walk, and bicycle allowed or not. Step-free stays
**not supported** and says so, because the feed does not carry it — a claim to avoid rather than a
feature to fake.
**Where.** The search, the preference model, and the line under the results that already admits what
is missing.
**Data.** None. Modes and changes are in the timetable; walking speed is arithmetic.
**Size.** M.
**Done when.** Turning trains off returns journeys with no train in them; asking for at most one
change returns journeys with at most one; and every preference the search ignores is greyed with a
reason rather than left looking applied.

### 5. An address, a postcode, or a place — not only a station

**9292:** from a door number to a door number.
**OVVO today:** stops only. Reverse geocoding exists (`src/lib/reverseGeocode.ts`, Nominatim, one
request per tap); forward search does not.

**Build.** A forward lookup that turns what was typed into a place: the stop index first, then an
address or a landmark, then the nearest stop that has services, with the distance stated. The
traveller sees which of the two they got.
**Where.** `src/lib/lookup`, through the app's own worker (`worker/lookup.js`) so the vendor is
called once per answer and the reply is cached at the edge.
**Data.** OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, free, no key, **with its usage policy honoured**: one request a
second, no per-keystroke autocomplete, an answer cached, and the attribution shown where the answer
is. If a bulk-friendly free geocoder can be verified instead, use it and record why.
**Size.** L.
**Done when.** A street and number in a town with no station in the app's 61 plans a journey, the
walk to the stop is its own leg with its own minutes, and the attribution is on screen.

### 6. Door to door: the walk at both ends

**9292:** the walk from your door to the platform, and from the platform to the door.
**OVVO today:** walks between stops within 400 m, and a foot route for a walk that already exists.

**Build.** The first and last walk as real foot routes from the address to the boarding stop, timed
by the router rather than by a straight line, and folded into the departure minute so the alarm and
the "leave by" minute are right.
**Where.** `src/lib/streetMap.ts` already does the routing and the caching; the search and the
journey page need to use it for the ends.
**Data.** OpenStreetMap's foot router, free, no key.
**Size.** L.
**Done when.** A journey from an address starts with a timed walk to the stop, the walk follows
streets, and the total journey time includes it.

### 7. The line, where the line actually runs

**9292:** the route is drawn on the real track and road.
**OVVO today:** a path through the stops; `shapes.txt` is unread.

**Build.** Read `shapes.txt` in the graph builder, keep one decimated polyline per pattern (a point
every fifty metres is plenty at phone zoom), and draw it under the journey and on the journey's map.
**Where.** `scripts/gtfs/build-graph.mjs`, a new static geometry file, the map and the journey
thumbnail.
**Data.** Already in the archive.
**Size.** M.
**Done when.** A train journey's line follows the railway and not a straight hop between stations,
and a bus journey's line follows the roads it is timetabled on. Checked on one long journey and one
short one, against the same journey in 9292.

### 8. Live delays on the journey, and the platform change and the cancellation too

**9292:** a cancelled train is cancelled on the screen.
**OVVO today:** a delay in seconds, per trip, drawn beside the planned time. That is all
(`/api/live`).

**Build.** Read the rest of what the realtime feed already carries for each stop of each trip: a
skipped stop, a cancelled trip, and a platform that differs from the timetable's. Draw each where it
belongs — the leg, the row, the rail — and never infer one from a similar-looking service number.
**Where.** `shared/live-feed.mjs` (the decoder), `worker/live.js` (the route), the journey page.
**Data.** The same national realtime feed already being read.
**Size.** M.
**Done when.** A cancelled trip shows as cancelled and not as on time; a skipped stop is not offered
as a place to get off; a platform that the feed moves is moved on screen with the timetable's
platform still visible behind it.

### 9. Real service alerts, instead of the invented ones

**9292:** what is actually wrong today, on the line you are looking at.
**OVVO today:** `src/data/disruptions.ts`, every alert a simulated example.

**Build.** Read the national alert feed (823 messages on 14 November), decode its header, its period
and what it is about — a stop, a line, a trip — and show those. Keep the demonstration list behind
`SAMPLE_DATA_ALLOWED`, where it already belongs, and never mix the two. An alert the feed does not
name is not shown; a feed that cannot be read says so.
**Where.** A new module beside `shared/live-feed.mjs`, a `/api/alerts` route, the disruptions screens
and the journey's alert row.
**Data.** `gtfs.ovapi.nl/nl/alerts.pb`, free, no key, already reachable from the worker.
**Size.** L.
**Done when.** The disruptions screen lists only alerts the feed is currently carrying, each opens
with the feed's own words, an expired one disappears, and a journey shows the alerts that touch its
own stops and lines and no others.

### 10. Plan it again when the leg is cancelled

**9292:** offers another way when yours falls over.
**OVVO today:** shows the delay and stops there.

**Build.** When a leg is cancelled or a change becomes impossible, search again from the stop you are
standing at, at the minute you are standing there, and offer the alternatives above the journey that
broke. One button, one answer, no invented options.
**Where.** The journey page, over the same search.
**Data.** None beyond what steps 8 and 9 bring.
**Size.** L.
**Done when.** A journey with a cancelled leg offers at least one alternative that does not use that
leg, and says plainly when there is none.

### 11. A price, if there is an honest one to show

**9292:** the fare, before you travel. **Not in the four he accepted** — so it stays in scope, with a
condition: only if a free published source exists.
**OVVO today:** `fare: { eur: 0, label: '' }`, deliberately empty.

**Build, in this order, and stop where the truth stops.**

1. Confirm what the archive actually carries: it has two `ticketing_*.txt` files and **no
   `fare_attributes.txt`**, so there is no fare in the timetable and computing one from it is
   impossible by construction.
2. Look for a published, machine-readable tariff: the national OV-chipkaart rate and each carrier's
   own published rates (NS publishes its own). Verify the date of the rate and the distance rule.
3. If a published rule exists, compute the fare per leg and label it as computed, with its source and
   its date, and say when the journey crosses carriers.
4. If no published rule can be verified, **show nothing** and say fares are not available. That is a
   finished outcome, not a failure.

**Where.** A fare module fed by a checked table, and the chip the journey page already has a place
for.
**Data.** The carriers' own published rates, if they exist in a form that can be read.
**Size.** M to find out; L to build it properly.
**Done when.** Either a fare appears with its source and its date on the journey page, or the page
says fares are not available — and no number anywhere is a guess dressed as a price.

### 12. A month of timetable, rebuilt without a person

**9292:** always the current timetable, including next month.
**OVVO today:** a week built by hand in a sandbox, and the feed's dates end on 12 December 2026.

**Build.** A scheduled job that fetches the newest archive, validates it, builds today plus six days,
publishes the graph files and prunes the days that have passed — so the graph never grows past the
host's file limits and the planner never offers a day it cannot plan. The pipeline already refuses a
broken archive and keeps the last good copy; this step only takes the hand off it.
**Where.** `scripts/gtfs/fetch.mjs`, `build-all-days.mjs`, a scheduled workflow that runs the pair
and deploys.
**Data.** The same free feed, which updates nightly.
**Size.** M.
**Done when.** Two consecutive weeks pass with nobody touching it, tomorrow is always planned, the
day after the feed's last date says so in words, and a failed run leaves yesterday's graph serving.

### 13. Long journeys: the thirty-minute miss, and the ten milliseconds

**9292:** answers a cross-country journey correctly and quickly.
**OVVO today:** 12–16 ms for the longest journeys against a 10 ms free allowance, and Utrecht
Centraal → Groningen arrives **30 minutes late** against the reference search.

**Build.** Two separate jobs, in this order, because the second is worthless while the first is open.

1. **Find the miss.** Make the fast search record the path it took, leg by leg, the way the reference
   scan now does. Compare the two paths on that one journey. The investigation already rules out
   rounds, candidate trips, transfer time and pruning, so this is a boarding the fast search never
   considers — and a recording will show it in one run where tuning has failed five times.
2. **Then the cost.** Only once the answer is right: a two-way search that meets in the middle, or a
   narrower scan that visits only what it has reached. If neither brings the longest journeys under
   the allowance, the honest end is that the longest journeys run on a free runtime with a larger
   allowance, and this file says so.

**Where.** `src/planner/graph.ts` and `scripts/routing/raptor.mjs`, which must stay in step.
**Data.** None.
**Size.** L, and honestly open-ended until the recording exists.
**Done when.** The ten golden journeys in `scripts/routing/golden.mjs` agree with the reference
search, that Groningen journey included, and the processor cost of each is recorded in this file.

### 14. The vehicles, on the map

**9292:** where the bus actually is.
**OVVO today:** nowhere. The vehicle feed was decoded once, in a probe, and is not in the app.

**Build.** Read the national vehicle feed, match each vehicle to a trip the app is showing by the
feed's own trip id, and draw it on the map with its age stated. A vehicle the feed does not name is
not drawn.
**Where.** A `/api/vehicles` route beside `/api/live`, and the map.
**Data.** `gtfs.ovapi.nl/nl/vehiclePositions.pb`, free, no key.
**Size.** M.
**Done when.** Opening the map on a live journey shows the vehicle moving, and a feed that has gone
quiet shows the last known position with the minute it was seen, not a moving dot pretending to be
now.

## The order to do it in

Chosen by what a traveller notices first, not by what is easiest to build.

1. **The picker and the boards** — 1, then 2. Until these are done, most of the country cannot use
   the app at all, and every other step is an improvement to a product most people cannot reach.
2. **The question itself** — 3, then 4. Arriving by a time, and preferences that are true.
3. **What is happening now** — 8, then 9, then 10. Delays, alerts, and a way round a cancellation.
4. **From a door** — 6, then 5. The walk at the ends first, because it is the shorter build, then the
   address lookup that feeds it.
5. **The picture** — 7. The line where the line runs.
6. **The price** — 11, and only as far as a verified source goes.
7. **Keeping it alive** — 12, which should really be pulled forward if the calendar is close to
   12 December 2026.
8. **The hard two** — 13, then 14.

Each group is built, checked at a phone width in both themes, deployed and pushed before the next
group starts. Nothing in this list is a rewrite: every step adds a source or a capability to code
that already exists.

## How we will know it is at parity

Not by a feeling. By sitting with 9292 and OVVO side by side and comparing twelve things:

1. A journey between two villages, neither of them in the app today.
2. A journey from a street address to a street address.
3. The same journey asked for by arrival time instead of departure time.
4. A journey on a Sunday, and one on a public holiday.
5. A journey after midnight, and one across the clock change.
6. A board at a small stop, at 07:00.
7. A board at a large station, at 07:00, compared row by row for platform and time.
8. A journey on a line with a live delay, compared against the operator's own app.
9. A journey on a line with a cancellation.
10. A journey whose line has an alert against it.
11. A long cross-country journey, timed with a stopwatch in both.
12. The same journey planned twice, a minute apart, to see the live data move.

When all twelve answer the same way in both, the data and the planning are at parity. Anything that
still differs is written into this file with the reason, not left to be discovered by the steerer.

## What will still be missing when all fourteen are done

Said plainly, so nobody expects them at the end:

- **The four he accepted**: e-tickets, OV-chipkaart balance, subscription advice, operator crowding.
- **9292's own data.** 9292 is a carrier-owned company with data and agreements OVVO will never hold.
  Its journey planner is a licensed product (`Reisadvies API`), and its terms forbid using it without
  approval, so it is not a source and will not be scraped.
- **International tickets and advice.** Times can be planned where a free feed covers them; selling
  the journey cannot.
- **A guaranteed fresh timetable beyond the feed's last date.** The feed publishes about four weeks;
  a day past it is honestly unavailable, not quietly extended.

---

## What this costs

Nothing. Every source named here is free and needs no account: the national timetable and its three
live feeds, OpenStreetMap's tiles, foot router and geocoder, the static hosting already paid for by
being free, and a scheduled job on the repository's own free allowance.

The only real cost is **weight on the traveller's phone**, and it will be measured rather than
assumed: one new service day is about 17 MB raw and roughly 6 MB compressed, and a repeat search on
the same day costs nothing because the timetable is kept on the device.

---

## What this plan will not do

- No paid service, no card, no trial.
- No scraping 9292 or any carrier's private interface.
- No invented delay, platform, alert, fare, or journey in a production build.
- No preference shown as applied when the search ignored it.
- No step ticked because its screen exists. A step is done when its **Done when** line has been run
  and passed, and this file records the run.
