OVVO, a Dutch public transport planner by VibeCreations
======================================================

This file exists for anything that reads over HTTP and does not run JavaScript: a link
preview, a crawler, a review bot, a text extractor. The app itself is drawn entirely by
JavaScript, so a raw fetch of the page cannot show it. This is the honest substitute.

The app: https://ovvo.vibecreations.workers.dev/
Support: ovvo.vibecreations@outlook.com
Made by VibeCreations. No custom domain, no payment card, free tier only, by decision.


What OVVO is
------------

A journey planner for Dutch public transport, in the spirit of 9292, built as a fast web app
that also installs to a phone's home screen and keeps working offline.

Timetables and disruptions in this build are clearly labelled demonstration data. OVVO does not
pretend to be a live source, and every screen that shows times says so where it matters. The
stop names, lines and places are real Dutch ones.


The screens
-----------

Home        A search for a journey from A to B, with the traveller's favourite stops, recent
            searches and the departure board they have pinned.

Results     Departures for a search, with duration, transfers, the lines used, the platform and
            any delay. A journey can be opened, saved, or its stops opened one by one.

Board       Live-style departure rows for one stop, sorted by time, with the platform, the
            direction and the delay. A board can be pinned to the home screen.

Stop        One stop: its lines, its board, a small map, and an encyclopaedia paragraph about
            the place from Wikipedia in the traveller's own language.

Journey     One journey in full: each leg, the walking parts, the changes, the times, and the
            line's own colour.

Search      Address and stop search. Addresses come from OpenStreetMap, stops from the app's own
            list, and the two are drawn together in one picker.

Themes      Fifty looks, each with its own page colour, ink, lettering and card treatment, plus
            a photograph behind the page if the traveller wants one. Seasonal looks appear in
            their own three weeks.

Settings    Appearance, language (Dutch and English), clock format, location permission,
            battery saving, names for favourite stops, developer panel, this account, the reset,
            and the support address at the foot.

Wrapped     A yearly review of the traveller's own year: a deck of chapters built only from what
            this app recorded (searches, boards, hearts, looks), never a made-up number and never
            a comparison with other travellers. It opens in its season and is kept afterwards.

Sign in     Four ways in, all free: Google, an email and password, a link by email, or a
            passkey. The sign-in mail is sent through Brevo from ovvo.vibecreations@outlook.com
            so it lands in the inbox rather than in spam. A passkey sign-in needs no email at all.


What is kept, and where
-----------------------

Without an account, everything stays in the browser. With an OVVO ID, the traveller's stops,
journeys, searches, settings and profile photo belong to the account and follow them to another
device; the browser keeps a copy so the app still works offline.

A photo is about thirty kilobytes, squared by the app before it is sent, and stored with the
account. Facial recognition, no advertising, no third-party analytics that follows a person: the
visitor counter is Cloudflare's, cookie-free, and counts visits rather than people.

Passkeys are checked by OVVO's own Worker against the public half of the key. Making an account
with a passkey passes a Cloudflare Turnstile check first, because that is the only door that asks
for no email and no password.


Reading the app with a browser
------------------------------

If a tool needs the rendered interface, it needs a real JavaScript engine: Playwright, Puppeteer,
Selenium or a headless Chromium all work. The page also accepts ?bypass_compat=1 (or ?compat=off,
or ?debug=1) and waves driven browsers past its old-browser notice by itself, since that notice
exists for old phones and not for reviewers. The branch the notice took is recorded on the
document element as data-compat: bypassed, automation, no-modules, yes, unknown or no.
