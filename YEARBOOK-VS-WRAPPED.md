## The size of the thing

Your capture is a **shorter cut**: 2 minutes 14 seconds, 8,064 frames. A full 2024 Wrapped is **14
parts, seven to eight minutes**, so your edit kept about nine story cards and trimmed the rest. The
parts that were cut are the parts that make Wrapped feel bigger than a film, so this file is in two
halves: what your video shows, and what the full run has according to Spotify's own 2024 material
(marked _listed, not pictured_ where I have no frame).

Our side: the portrait recording of the Yearbook in the repo
(`design/wrapped-comparison/yearbook-portrait.webm`, 390 × 844), read at one frame a second too. It was
recorded with sound off and tapped through by hand, so its pacing is mine, not the film's.

What I could not check: the sound. The file has an audio track and I cannot listen to it.

### The gaps you can see in that cut

**1. The small line under the big one.** This is the biggest thing I missed first time. Every card says
something big, then adds a smaller line underneath a beat later: "You listened for 7,145 minutes" →
"And you made each one count."; "Coldplay was your top artist" → "Your longest listening streak was 5
days."; "You're a top 0.5% fan" → "You overachiever." That second line is where the warmth is. Our rule
is one sentence per chapter, so every chapter stops at its first thought. **A rule decision for you,
not a bug.**

**2. A pause.** Their header has pause beside the mute, because the film runs itself. Ours runs itself
too — the score is the clock — but there is no way to hold a card. When a line lands, your only options
are the sound switch and the X.

**3. A ranked list, with artwork.** A card of its own — "Your top artists", numbered, with cover
pictures, five rows that scroll inside it — and then ten of them printed on the closing card. We name
one line and one route and never put anything in order. This is still the biggest content gap.

**4. A rank among other people.** "You're a top 0.5% fan." One number against everyone else, and it is
the line people screenshot. We compare the traveller to themselves.

**5. A single date.** "Your biggest listening day was October 24 with 340 minutes." One day, named, with
a number on it. We aggregate the year and never pick out a date.

**6. A streak.** "Your longest listening streak was 5 days." Cheap from journey data, and it turns a
total into a habit. We have no streak at all.

**7. Real footage.** Fifteen seconds of the actual band, full-bleed, no words on it. We have one
photograph in eighteen chapters and geometry everywhere else.

**8. The closing card is the share card.** Theirs ends on the thing you send, in eight colourways you
page through, with Share under it and "Replay from beginning" below. Ours is a good sheet — three cards
plus one per award, hidden station names if you like, previewed at 1080 × 1920 — but it is somewhere you
go from a pill, not the end of the story, and there is no rank on it to print.

**9. Progress you can read.** One dash per card across the top, and a rail down the left that moves with
you. Our eighteen dashes are hairlines under the Share pill, so the film feels endless and the ending is
a surprise.

**10. Objects with depth.** Theirs are continuous 3D renders with stepped edges and real glow, cropped
by the frame. Ours are lit three.js bodies, but one simple form each. The smallest of the ten.

## What the Yearbook has that Wrapped doesn't

- **Eighteen chapters against their fourteen** — more, but only just. My last version claimed this as a
  win by counting their edit instead of their run. That was wrong.
- **Geography.** Wrapped 2024 has no map and no places at all. Our nine places across a blue field is
  something Wrapped cannot do.
- **A journey you can name.** "IC 3100. Your main character line." is concrete in a way "1,742 minutes
  together" is not.
- **A year against the year before.** "+32 journeys. Show-off." Their film never compares you to
  yourself.
- **A score of our own, one piece per chapter,** and the song keeps the time.
- **Nothing to sign into, nothing uploaded.** Their share card carries their address; ours is drawn on
  the device.
- **One calm sentence per chapter** — the reason the film reads as a film and not a dashboard.

## If only three things get built

1. **A ranked five with small pictures.** Five most-visited stations or lines, numbered, each with a
   picture. It becomes the chapter people screenshot, and it gives the share card something to print.
2. **The small line under the big one.** A quieter second sentence on each chapter, a beat later. It is
   their warmest move and our rule forbids it — your call.
3. **End on the share card.** Show it full screen on the closing chapter, in three colourways, with
   Share under it, instead of a sheet fetched from a pill.

Then, in order: a busiest day ("Your busiest day was 14 March, 9 journeys"), a streak ("You travelled 11
days in a row in October"), a pause button, and one chapter with real footage in it.
