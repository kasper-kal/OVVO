# What Spotify Wrapped has that the Yearbook doesn't

Written 17 November 2026, from the steerer's own capture of Wrapped 2024.

## First, the size of the thing

**Your video is a shorter cut.** It is 2 minutes 14 seconds, 8,064 frames, and I watched all of it a
second at a time. The full 2024 Wrapped is **14 parts and runs about seven to eight minutes**, so your
edit kept roughly ten moments and trimmed the rest — probably to fit it on a phone.

That matters, because the parts that were cut are exactly the parts that make Wrapped feel bigger than
a film. So this file is in two halves: what I actually saw in your video, and what the whole experience
has according to Spotify's own 2024 announcement. The second half is marked _(listed, not pictured)_
wherever I have no frame for it.

**Our side.** The portrait recording of the Yearbook already in the repo
(`design/wrapped-comparison/yearbook-portrait.webm`, 390 × 844), read at the same one frame per second.
It was recorded with sound off and tapped through by hand, so its pacing is mine, not the film's.

**What I could not check.** The sound. Your file carries an audio track, but I cannot listen to it.

### The gaps you can see in that cut

**1. Two beats on one card.** Wrapped says one thing, waits three or four seconds, then lands a second
line on the same card: "You listened for 7,145 minutes" → "And you made each one count."; "Coldplay was
your top artist" → "Your longest listening streak was 5 days."; "You're a top 0.5% fan" → "You
overachiever." The second line is where the feeling is. Our one-sentence rule means every chapter stops
at its first thought. **This is a rule decision for you, not a bug.**

**2. A pause.** Their header has pause beside the mute, because the film runs on its own. Ours runs on
its own too — the score is the clock — but there is no way to hold a card still. When a sentence lands,
your only options are the sound switch and the X.

**3. A ranked five, with pictures.** Top five artists, then top five songs, numbered, each with cover
art, each name real. We name one line and one route and never put anything in order. Still the biggest
content gap: a year with five favourite stations beats a year with one.

**4. A rank among other people.** "You're a top 0.5% fan." One number against everyone else, and it is
the sentence people screenshot. We compare the traveller to themselves (47% unchanged, +32 journeys).

**5. A single date.** "Your biggest listening day was October 24 with 340 minutes." One day, named, with
a number on it. We aggregate the year and never pick out a date.

**6. A streak.** "Your longest listening streak was 5 days." Cheap to compute from journey data, and it
turns a total into a habit. We have no streak at all.

**7. Real footage of a real thing.** Fourteen seconds of the actual band, full-bleed. We have one
photograph in eighteen chapters (Utrecht Centraal at night) and geometry everywhere else.

**8. A share card that is the last chapter.** Their closing screen *is* the card — top five artists, top
five songs, minutes, address — in eight colourways you page through, then Share, then "Replay from
beginning". Ours is a good sheet (three cards plus one per award, station names hidden if you like,
previewed at 1080 × 1920) but it is somewhere you go, not the end of the story, and with no ranked list
there is nothing on it that says what you loved most.

**9. Progress you can read at a glance.** One dash per card across the top, plus a rail down the left
that moves with you. Our eighteen dashes are hairlines under the Share pill, so from arm's length the
film feels endless and the ending is a surprise.

**10. Objects with depth.** Theirs are pixel-stepped, layered, glowing, cropped hard by the frame.
Ours are lit three.js bodies, but one simple form each — a band, a cube, a ring, a cylinder. The
smallest of the ten, and the least worth a week.

## B. What the full seven-to-eight-minute run has _(listed, not pictured)_

These are from Spotify's own 2024 announcement and press coverage, not from your video. Your edit cut
them to save space, so I could not see them.

**A story for each kind of listening.** The cut keeps minutes, artists and the top artist. The full run
also has a top song card, a top podcast card with its own cover art, and a podcast topic card. One
medium each, in its own art direction.

**Music Evolution.** Up to three musical phases across the year, each with descriptors, genres and
artists, plus a playlist built from them. It is the "3 eras. A full character arc." idea, done with
three cards and real names instead of one line. We have the one line.

**Artist and podcaster clips.** Not just the top artist — several of your top artists and podcasters
record a short message that plays inside your Wrapped.

**An AI podcast about your year.** Two synthetic hosts talking through your top songs, artists and
genres, made with Google's NotebookLM, minutes long, listenable on a walk. Nothing of ours is
listenable.

**An AI DJ who talks you through it.** A voice between the songs, telling you about your year and
offering editorial about the big tracks.

**A hub.** Wrapped has a feed on the Home screen that holds the film, the playlists, the editorial and
the extra insights. One place to start, and one place to come back to. We have a cover with Begin.

**Sharing everywhere.** "Share this story" on every card; a Wrapped spin on the normal share button that
tells you where a song sits in your top 100; and direct export to TikTok and Instagram.

**Fourteen parts.** Each one gets long enough to breathe, because there are fourteen, not fourteen
hundred. Our eighteen chapters are shorter per chapter than theirs are per part.

**On a speaker, too.** "Play top songs 2024" on a smart speaker.

## What the Yearbook has that Wrapped doesn't

- **More chapters, not more story.** Eighteen against their fourteen. That is close, and it is not the
  win I claimed in the last version of this file. I had counted their edit instead of their run.
- **Geography.** Wrapped 2024 has no map and no places at all — they even dropped Sound Town this year.
  Our "9 places. You got around." with the dots across a blue field is something Wrapped cannot do.
- **A journey you can name.** "IC 3100. Your main character line." and "20 times: Amsterdam ↔ Haarlem"
  are concrete in a way "1,742 minutes together" is not.
- **A year against the year before.** "+32 journeys. Show-off." Their film never compares you to
  yourself.
- **A score of our own, one piece per chapter,** and the song keeps the time. Theirs is one bed under
  the whole thing.
- **Nothing to sign into, nothing uploaded.** Their share card carries their address; ours is drawn on
  the device.
- **One calm sentence per chapter,** which is why the film reads like a film and not a dashboard.

## If only three things get built

1. **A ranked five, with small pictures.** Five most-visited stations or lines, numbered, each with a
   small picture. One chapter becomes the chapter people screenshot, and it fills the share card.
2. **A hub and a way back.** A small sheet of the eighteen chapters before the film, and an arrow that
   steps back one. Their feed is why you can browse; our film is why you can't.
3. **The share card on the closing chapter.** Show it full screen like theirs, in three colourways, with
   Share under it — instead of a sheet you fetch from a pill.

Then, in order after those: a single biggest day ("Your busiest day was 14 March, 9 journeys"), a
streak ("You travelled 11 days in a row in October"), and one chapter with real footage in it.

The two-beat card from their grammar is the one I would put to you rather than build: it breaks our
one-sentence rule, and that rule is the reason the film is calm.
