# What Spotify Wrapped has that the Yearbook doesn't

Written 17 November 2026, after watching both — the real thing this time.

## How this was watched

**Wrapped.** Your file, `VID_20261102_013903_1.mp4`: 134 seconds, 860 × 1920, 60 frames a second,
8,064 frames, with an audio track. I pulled one frame per second — 134 frames — laid them out in 17
contact sheets and read every sheet, then opened full-size frames wherever text was the point. So the
notes below are from your capture, not from press shots.

**The Yearbook.** The portrait recording of our own film already in the repo
(`design/wrapped-comparison/yearbook-portrait.webm`, 390 × 844). I pulled it at the same one frame per
second and read it the same way, so the two sides are like for like.

**This replaces the earlier file.** The version before this one compared against Spotify's published
frames of *2025* Wrapped, which is a different experience: it has a hub, a back arrow and a speed
control. Your video is 2024, it has none of those, it starts straight into the story, and it has other
things 2025 dropped. Everything below is only what is in your video.

**What I could not check.** The sound. Your file carries audio, but I cannot listen to it, so I can
only say a Wrapped film plays with a soundtrack and a mute button, not what the music was.

**One thing about our own recording.** It was taken with sound off and tapped through by hand, so the
pacing in it is mine, not the film's. The Yearbook does turn its own pages: the score is the clock, and
a silent timer covers the same ground when the music is off. The notes below are corrected for that —
I had two of them wrong the first time round.

## 1. A pause

Wrapped carries a **pause** button beside the mute, because the film runs on its own. Ours runs on its
own too — each chapter's song is its clock and turns the page when it ends, and a timer does the same
in silence. So what we are missing is not the running, it is the opposite control: there is no way to
hold a card on screen. When a sentence lands and you want to sit with it, your only options are the
sound switch and the X.

Smaller, and also real: Wrapped starts on opening the page. Ours needs a press of Begin, because a
phone will not play the opening music until a thumb has touched the screen.

## 2. Two beats on one card

This is the thing I had wrong before. Wrapped does not say one thing per card — it says one thing,
waits three or four seconds, then lands the second line on the same card:

- "You listened for 7,145 minutes this year" → "And you made each one count."
- "Coldplay was your top artist of the year" → "Your longest listening streak was 5 days."
- "You're a top 0.5% fan…" → "You overachiever."

The second line is where the feeling is. Our rule — one sentence, one size, still — is stricter than
Wrapped's own. That is a deliberate choice, but it means every one of our chapters ends on its first
thought and never gets the punchline.

## 3. A ranked five, with artwork

Wrapped gives you the top five **artists** and then the top five **songs**, numbered, each with its
cover picture, each name real. The Yearbook names one line and one route and never puts anything in
order. This is still the biggest content gap: a year with five favourite stations is more interesting
than a year with one.

## 4. A rank among other people

"You're a top 0.5% fan." One number that puts you against everyone else, and it is the sentence people
screenshot. Ours describes you to yourself (47% of journeys unchanged, +32 journeys); nothing places
you against the rest of the network.

## 5. A single date

"Your biggest listening day was October 24 with 340 minutes." One day, named, with a number on it. The
Yearbook aggregates the whole year and never picks out a date. People remember dates.

## 6. A streak

"Your longest listening streak was 5 days." A tiny run of days, which costs nothing to compute from
journey data and turns a total into a habit. We have no streak of any kind.

## 7. Real footage of a real thing

Fourteen seconds of the actual band, full-bleed, silent, just people standing there. The Yearbook has
one photograph in eighteen chapters (Utrecht Centraal at night) and otherwise abstract geometry. Even
a five-second loop of a platform at rush hour would do more emotional work than another lit shape.

## 8. A share card that is the last chapter

The last screen *is* the share card: your top five artists, your top five songs, the minutes, the
address of the experience, in eight colourways paged with the dots underneath, then Share, then
"Replay from beginning". You see exactly what you are about to send, and the card is built out of your
ranked five.

Ours is a decent sheet — three cards (who you were, your route, the year in numbers) plus one per
award, a switch to hide station names, drawn at 1080 × 1920 and previewed before it is saved. But it
is somewhere you go, not the end of the story, and with no ranked list in the film there is nothing on
it that says what you loved most. Two pieces of work, not one: show the card on the closing chapter,
and give it a top five worth printing.

## 9. Progress you can read at a glance

Wrapped puts one dash per card along the top edge and a rail down the left side that moves as you do,
so you always know how far in you are and how much is left. Our eighteen progress dashes are hairlines
tucked under the Share pill at the bottom. From arm's length the film feels endless, and the ending is
a surprise rather than something you saw coming.

## 10. Objects with depth

Wrapped's shapes are pixel-stepped, layered, glowing, cropped hard by the frame. Our masses are lit
three.js bodies, which was the right move, but each chapter still carries one simple form — a band, a
cube, a ring, a cylinder. Theirs feel photographed; ours feel modelled. This is the smallest of the
ten, and the least worth spending a week on.

## What the Yearbook has that Wrapped doesn't

Worth saying out loud, because it is the reason not to copy it wholesale:

- **More story, not less.** Eighteen chapters against their nine, so there is room for the departure
  hour, the line, the route pair and the comparison to last year in separate beats.
- **Geography.** Wrapped 2024 has no map and no places at all. Our "9 places. You got around." with the
  dots across a blue field is something Wrapped cannot do.
- **A journey you can name.** "IC 3100. Your main character line." and "20 times: Amsterdam ↔ Haarlem"
  are concrete in a way "1,742 minutes together" is not.
- **A year against the year before.** "+32 journeys. Show-off." Their film never compares you to
  yourself.
- **Nothing to sign into, nothing uploaded.** Their share card carries their address; ours is drawn on
  the device.
- **A score of our own, and one piece per chapter.** Theirs is a single bed under the whole thing; ours
  changes song at every turn, and the song is what keeps the time. That is more film-like than theirs,
  and it is the reason the deck already plays itself.

## If only three things get built

1. **A ranked five, with small pictures.** Five most-visited stations or lines, numbered. One chapter
   becomes the chapter people screenshot.
2. **A pause.** One button beside the sound switch that holds the chapter and its music where they
   are. The film already runs itself; it just cannot be stopped mid-sentence.
3. **Show the share card before sending it.** Open the share sheet on the card itself, in three
   colourways, so what you are about to send is what you are looking at.

The next two after those, in order: a single biggest day ("Your busiest day was 14 March, 9 journeys")
and a streak ("You travelled 11 days in a row in October").
