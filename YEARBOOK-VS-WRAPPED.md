# What Wrapped has that the Yearbook doesn't

Written 17 November 2026, after watching both, frame by frame.

## How this was watched

**The Yearbook.** I recorded it: a real 40-second portrait capture of the live build
(`design/wrapped-comparison/yearbook-portrait.webm`, 390 × 844), walked from the cover through all
18 chapters with a pause on each. I pulled every frame out of that recording (1,001 of them) and read
a sampled sheet of 41 — the whole film, not a spot check
(`design/wrapped-comparison/yearbook-frames.png`). Both are committed, so you can play the film back
without running anything.

**Wrapped.** I have no Spotify account in this workspace, so I did not record a personal Wrapped. I
used Spotify's own published frames of the 2025 experience instead — the minutes, genres, podcasts,
audiobooks, entry points and share-card sets — plus Spotify's card-by-card list of what is inside
the 2025 experience, and a published walkthrough that describes the parts the marketing frames don't
show (the quiz, the sprint, the highlight reports). Those reference images are Spotify's, so they are
kept in `artifacts/wrapped-recording/wrapped-ref/` and not committed.

So: our side is a recording; their side is the official published screens and the official list. That
is the honest limit of this comparison. Where something below is described but not visible in a
frame, it is marked _(listed, not pictured)_.

---

## 1. The story it tells

**Ranked lists, with the things named and pictured.** Wrapped gives you your top five songs, artists,
albums, podcasts and genres — each one a real name, each one with its own artwork, in order, numbered
1 to 5. The Yearbook names one line ("IC 3100. Your main character line.") and one route ("17 times:
Amsterdam ↔ Haarlem"), and never shows a ranked list of anything. This is the single biggest content
gap: a year with five favourite stations is more interesting than a year with one.

**A rank among other people.** "You streamed 2,349 minutes and you're a top 0.1% fan." It is a
sentence people screenshot. We have percentages that describe the traveller to themselves (47% of
journeys unchanged, +32 journeys), but nothing that places them against everyone else on the network.

**A number that is absurd and true.** "78,432 minutes. That's 86 days. Nice." One figure, converted
into a unit a person can feel, then undercut with one word. Our best attempt is "7,291 km. That's a
lot of window-gazing." — close, but we do it once in eighteen chapters.

**Day-level highlights.** Up to five small AI-written reports: your most nostalgic day, your biggest
listening day, the day your taste went wide. Ours aggregates the year; it never picks a single date
and says "this one".

**A club you belong to.** Wrapped sorts you into one of six clubs, each with its own name, and tells
you the role you play in it. Ours describes a habit (names, hours, distance) but never gives the
traveller a group to belong to or a label to repeat.

**A human voice thanking you.** An artist, podcaster or author records a short clip that plays inside
your own Wrapped. We have no voice of any kind, human or otherwise.

## 2. The shape of the experience

**A hub before the film.** Wrapped opens on a feed with tiles — Your Stories, Wrapped Party, this
year in music/podcasts/audiobooks, your playlist — and a "Let's go" card. The Yearbook opens on the
cover and only goes forward. There is nowhere to browse what you are about to see, and nowhere to
come back to afterwards.

**Going back.** Wrapped has a back arrow on every card, and its 2025 release added the ability to
revisit a moment without replaying from the start. Our film has one direction, a close button and a
row of progress dashes. If you liked chapter 7 you cannot return to it.

**A speed control.** Wrapped 2025 lets you change how fast the experience plays. Ours is fixed and
manual: one press per chapter, no autoplay, no pace setting.

**Share on every story, in more than one shape.** Wrapped puts "Share this story" on each card, makes
the card in portrait and landscape, and can send it straight into its own messaging. We do have
share cards — five layouts at 1080 × 1920, built in the browser — but they are a separate sheet you
open from a fixed button, not a card that belongs to the chapter you are standing on, and only in
story size.

**An ending that hands you something.** Wrapped ends by putting your top songs in a playlist, opening
Wrapped Party, and sending you back into the app. Ours ends on a sentence, a ring and "Start again" /
"Copy my year". The film stops; it doesn't hand over.

## 3. The craft

**Music.** Wrapped has a soundtrack that runs under the whole thing, and a mute button in the header
so you can turn it off. We have a mute button too — for sound effects. There is no music at all, and
sound is off by default.

**Real pictures of the things you loved.** Album covers, podcast covers, artist photos. The images do
most of the emotional work. Ours is abstract lit geometry with one photograph in eighteen chapters
(the station at night).

**Lines that carry between cards.** Wrapped draws a thin white squiggle that starts on one screen and
continues onto the next, so the cards feel like one sheet of paper being pulled. Our chapters are
hard cuts; the mass inside each one moves, but nothing connects one card to the next.

**A different art direction per story.** Black with lavender numerals for minutes, cream with red
discs for genres, olive and lime stripes for podcasts. Each story looks like its own poster. Ours
changes colour per chapter but keeps one grammar throughout.

**Type that performs.** Colossal numerals, labels in tape-like black blocks, overlapping words, text
that is part of the picture rather than sitting on it. Our rule (one sentence, one size, still) is
deliberate and it is what makes the film calm — but it is also why our cards read as quieter than
theirs at a glance.

## 4. The machine underneath

**Named people and things.** Wrapped can say "Good Hang with Amy Poehler" because it knows what you
listened to. We know stations, lines and times, and we use them — but only one per chapter, never as
a cast.

**Multiplayer.** Wrapped Party compares your year with friends live and hands out awards. There is
nothing social in the Yearbook at all. _(listed, not pictured)_

**A year-on-year archive.** Wrapped can look back across years. We have "3 eras" inside one year, and
"Your demo year is ready", but no memory of the year before.

**Eligibility and a reason to come back.** Wrapped needs 30 songs and 5 artists, and says so, which
turns it into an event with a door. We will show a yearbook for three journeys, which is welcoming
but never feels earned.

## What the Yearbook has that Wrapped doesn't

Worth saying, because it is the reason not to copy Wrapped wholesale:

- **It is about getting somewhere, not consuming.** A year of journeys, times, lines and kilometres
  is a real part of a life in a way a listening total is not.
- **It is local and private by default.** No account, no upload; the share cards are drawn in the
  browser and nothing leaves the device until the traveller sends one.
- **It has a printable, ticket-like object** — the award ticket card — that Wrapped has no equivalent
  for.
- **It keeps one calm sentence per chapter**, which is the thing that makes it feel like a film
  rather than a dashboard.

## If only three things get built

1. **A ranked five, with art.** Your five most-visited stations or lines, numbered, with a small
   picture of each. It turns one chapter into the chapter people screenshot.
2. **A rank among others.** "You're in the top 8% of travellers on this line." One sentence, and it
   is the sentence that gets shared.
3. **A way back and a hub.** A small index of the eighteen chapters before the film starts, and an
   arrow to step back. Cheap to build, and it stops the film being something you can only watch once.
